## Original problem statement

Large numbers of PCs, workstations, edge servers, and future embodied-intelligence terminals have idle time, but their capacity cannot become reliable supply without a shared standard for dispatch, checkpointing, verification, failure recovery, and accepted-result settlement.

## Original economic framing

DTVS does not claim that a distributed consumer GPU is intrinsically faster or more energy efficient than centralized infrastructure. It proposes asymmetric computation:

1. scarce high-context systems interpret, compile, schedule, and verify;
2. abundant low-context terminals execute bounded work units over longer deadlines;
3. time offsets performance, while redundancy and verification offset lower trust;
4. the existing device's capital cost is attributed to its primary use, while DTVS measures only marginal task cost plus a transparent node incentive;
5. tasks are accepted only when the full DTVS cost is below the centralized cost of an equivalent accepted result.
